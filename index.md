# Astrovia privacy policy

Last updated: October 31, 2026

Astrovia has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. It doesn't collect any personal data and has no server of its own that stores data about you.

## Your location

- If you allow it, Astrovia reads your phone's location only while the app is open. It is used on your phone to calculate the sky from where you are.
- Astrovia never stores your location on a server, and never sells or shares it for advertising.
- To find the name of your city, your phone asks its own service (Apple on iPhone, Google on Android).
- For the cloud forecast, Astrovia sends a rounded position (about 1 km) to Open-Meteo, which answers with the forecast for that area.
- You can say no, or turn location off at any time in your phone's settings, and choose a city by hand instead.

## Saved on your phone

If you choose a city by hand, it is saved on your phone so it is still there next time. Deleting the app deletes it.

## Services Astrovia connects to

- Open-Meteo: the cloud forecast and the city search (the name you type).
- Supabase: the object cards and the space missions.
- Wikimedia Commons, the news sources and the Spaceflight News API: pictures and articles.

Like any website, these services can see your IP address when the app asks them for data. Astrovia sends them nothing that identifies you. When you open a link, the page opens in a browser and that site's own privacy policy applies.

## Children

Astrovia collects no personal data from anyone, including children.

## Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published here with a new date.
